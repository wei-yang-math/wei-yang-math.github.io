## Abstract

We prove that $\operatorname{Mot}^{\mathrm{loc}}_R$ is not compactly generated
for every nonzero connective $\mathbb{E}_2$-ring spectrum $R$, removing the
nonzero-rationalization hypothesis from Ramzi's theorem. The new input is the
positive-characteristic case. For a perfect field $k$ of characteristic $p$,
we construct a $k$-linear motive whose cyclotomic topological Hochschild
homology is compact but not dualizable over $\operatorname{THH}(k)$. The
compact object comes from the one-weight polygonic cell, and its
nondualizability is detected by relative Tate assembly. Ramzi's universal
$\operatorname{THH}$-motive realizes the cell, while Efimov's rigidity theorem
converts the discrepancy into failure of compact generation. The general case
follows by passage to a perfect residue field, together with Ramzi's rational
result.
